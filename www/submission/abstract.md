# OpenReview abstract

**Title:** When Can a Bandit Trust Correlation? Spatial and Temporal Dependence in Web Bandits

**Suggested track:** Search, Recommendation, and Retrieval-Augmented AI

**Keywords:** multi-armed bandits; spatiotemporal dependence; graph bandits; restless bandits; autoregressive rewards; confidence sequences

Web platforms log graphs, such as co-engagement, embeddings and hyperlinks, that suggest which items share appeal, and their rewards persist and move together for days. Online recommenders that pool along such graphs, or that treat feedback as independent, can fail. We ask which logged correlation a bandit can trust and when its confidence statements break. Rewards are fixed long-run means plus autoregressive deviations with graph-correlated innovations, and a graph plays two separate roles: restricting the means or shaping the shocks. On the theory side, the regret of certified pooling depends on which components its certificate can reject, and graph geometry beyond diameters sets the information cost. An exact innovation regression gives confidence sets that are valid at all times under adaptive, restless sampling and keeps certified pooling valid. Confidence built for independent rewards fails under bursty exposure by an autocorrelation inflation factor. Each prediction is tested on KuaiRec, Wikipedia and the Open Bandit Dataset. Certified gains track the share of rejectable components (Spearman −0.87 across certificates on KuaiRec), while graph-free shrinkage performs strongly and several graph-specific comparisons are inconclusive. Held-out Wikipedia attention shows the predicted inflation: a week of views carries the information of 1.3–1.9 independent days. On worlds calibrated to it, iid certificates fail in 56–94% of runs, while innovation certificates show no failures but are too wide to eliminate. On chronological KuaiRec panels, persistence-aware policies halve loss against iid Thompson sampling, while comparisons with last observed value are inconclusive.
