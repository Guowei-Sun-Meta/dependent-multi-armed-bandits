\section{Updated empirical evidence: completed 10--11 October runs}
\label{sec:latest}

This section supersedes earlier empirical headlines where protocols or comparators have changed. It incorporates the completed 120-user KuaiRec expansion, the chronological daily rerun, the full-year historical Wikipedia extension, general-AR coverage confirmation, real-calibrated certificates, and the Open Bandit Dataset (OBD). The experiments finished on 10--11 October; this manuscript update is dated 12 October. Legacy numerical studies and derivations remain useful under their original assumptions.

\paragraph{What the evidence tests.} Static KuaiRec and the main OBD pooling study simulate Bernoulli feedback with real-data-derived means. The daily panels emulate a monitoring budget on an observed field. Controlled worlds identify permanent means, state-oracle regret and coverage separately. The OBD censored-log pilot uses actual randomized logged feedback. These are distinct estimands; no aggregate regret or probability-of-correct-selection (PCS) is formed across them. Known-model, oracle-width and fitted-model results also remain distinct. All comparisons below are exploratory and intervals are pointwise, without adjustment for the many policies and configurations examined.

\subsection{Mean-channel pooling: the completed 120-user expansion}

Setting A v2 now comprises 120 held-out users and three independently seeded video subsets per user: 360 distinct user--subset instances, 300 videos per instance and 20,000 pulls. The main phase contains 10,440 policy trajectories; the subsequent per-user-calibration phase adds 1,440 trajectories on the same instances. Thus the 720 logged phase completions are not 720 independent data instances. Feedback is simulated from the released reward matrix; common random numbers pair policies within each instance. The original 30-user, two-subset study is nested in this expansion, not an independent replication.

\paragraph{Two ratios answer different questions.} We report $\overline{R}_{\rm policy}/\overline{R}_{\rm baseline}$ for average loss, and preserve the original tables' $\overline{R_{\rm policy}/R_{\rm baseline}}$ as a separate measure of relative instance performance. Small baseline regrets can dominate the latter. For uncertainty and tail summaries, the three subsets are first averaged within each user, then 120 users are bootstrapped with 2,000 paired draws. These intervals condition on the common tuning population and the estimated reward matrix.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/pooling.tex}

Oracle-certified I-mf pooling has mean regret 0.640 times the matched fixed-confidence KL-UCB baseline, but 1.456 times Bernoulli TS. Its mean paired instance ratio to fixed KL-UCB is 0.802. These are compatible conclusions: certification helps within the conservative confidence-based family, while it does not establish superiority over a strong sampling baseline. Energy and global 90th-percentile widths remain wide; on I-mf their fractions of rejectable suboptimal components are 0.209 and 0.146, against 0.594 for oracle widths. The global 50th-percentile width is useful on average but covers only 47.3\% of I-mf components. A 90\% cross-user quantile is neither a 95\% simultaneous guarantee nor an anytime certificate for a new user. Per-user diagnostic coverage is evaluated at the true user level, whereas the policy selects widths after a noisy, paid warm-up; the diagnostic is not realized warm-up coverage.

\paragraph{Topology versus shrinkage.} Real I-mf and its degree-preserving rewiring have almost identical spectral-TS mean regret: relative difference $+0.29\%$, paired user interval $[-4.13\%,4.64\%]$. An interval crossing zero is inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence. Oracle-certified pooling gains $8.45\%$ over the rewired graph, interval $[7.38\%,9.46\%]$ improvement. Graph-free shrinkage TS has $13.39\%$ lower mean regret than real I-mf spectral TS, interval $[4.50\%,22.10\%]$ improvement. Specific topology therefore helps the oracle pooling geometry in this setting, while much of the uncensored smoothing gain is explained by shrinkage. Strong I-coeng spectral TS has a 90th-percentile user-averaged regret ratio of 4.70 to TS; 26.7\% of users exceed twice TS. Tail risk persists, but the older 18.78 instance-tail number belongs to a different cohort/subset design and is not the expansion's headline.

\subsection{Chronological KuaiRec: persistence survives, topology is unresolved}

The corrected daily study rebuilds co-engagement and IDF using only non-evaluation-user interactions before each test cutoff. Eligibility requires at least ten plays on every fitting day and does not condition on future survival. Five panels of 49--50 videos enter each of three disjoint 11-day windows, days 31--41, 42--52 and 53--63. Hyperparameters are selected on days 20--30 and then frozen. Five policy seeds are averaged within each of the 15 panel-window units. The source development panels are drawn using origin-30 eligibility and graph communities; development selection is therefore conditional on that cohort, although the held-out test cutoffs exclude future interactions. The source's policy RNG also uses Python's process-dependent hash, which should be replaced by a stable identifier for exact rerun reproducibility.

Availability is unknown before selection. A chosen video with no plays provides no observation. For retrospective scoring its slot receives the day's median available reward; the oracle selects the best available slate. This convention is part of the monitoring proxy, not an observed platform reward or an online treatment effect. Seventeen test cells are unavailable; 201 unavailable selections occur across 1,950 policy trajectories. Intervals resample the three windows and then their panels. With only three windows and shared global shocks/fits, they are conditional sensitivity summaries rather than broad platform confidence guarantees.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/daily.tex}

Joint UCB has 49.3\% less loss than iid TS at one selection/day and 54.1\% less at five, with relative-difference intervals $[-84.8\%,-13.4\%]$ and $[-67.0\%,-42.6\%]$. Against last observed value, however, its differences are $+3.2\%$ and $+6.6\%$, with intervals crossing zero. Joint greedy versus last value is likewise inconclusive. This supports persistence-aware decisions, but does not establish that a full fitted model outperforms the simplest persistence rule. At five selections/day, joint UCB improves on temporal-only UCB by 11.3\%, interval $[5.3\%,20.4\%]$ improvement. The real graph versus rewiring ($-1.4\%$) and versus a common factor ($-3.5\%$) are inconclusive. Correlated-state inference helps some comparisons; the actual co-engagement topology has no established incremental decision benefit here.

\subsection{Historical Wikipedia: the full-year extension is panel-dependent}

The historical astronomy and football panels each contain 24 fixed articles and pre-2024 hyperlink matrices. All policies receive the same complete 2024 initialization, 8,784 readings per panel, and selectively observe the 365-day 2025 field without a full-field test refresh. Policy hyperparameters are fixed using 2024 development data. The extension overlaps the earlier 181-day study and therefore cannot be counted as another independent Web history. Five stochastic seeds quantify algorithmic randomization conditional on each observed field; date-block sensitivities at 7, 14 and 28 days address temporal dependence within those fields.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/wikipedia.tex}

At five readings/day, astronomy joint UCB is 25.1\% below iid TS, but EWMA is only 2.6\% above joint UCB and their comparison is inconclusive. In football, temporal greedy is 40.9\% below iid TS and has lower loss than joint UCB. At one reading/day, static ranking and iid rules remain strong. These results replace any statement that full temporal modelling or graph filtering wins on every panel or budget. The older 150-article one-domain and six-community Wikipedia panels used retrospective popularity/completeness selection and current hyperlink snapshots; their shock-correlation and policy results remain diagnostics, not chronological replications.

\subsection{Bridge validity, misspecification and the cost of useful decisions}

\paragraph{Fresh general-AR confirmation.} After a 480-run screen over AR(1), AR(7), AR(20) and heterogeneous orders, a selected AR(20) setting was confirmed on 100 fresh paired worlds per learner, with an independent fitting prefix. Known-model coverage is compatible with the nominal 5\% target; plug-in estimation and covariance inflation remain empirical heuristics. Wrong lag order and diagonal innovations are combined in this confirmation and cannot be attributed separately.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/coverage.tex}

Other robustness runs distinguish missed weekly lags and heavy tails: AR(7) truth fitted as AR(1) gives 20--73\% violations across policies, while variance-normalized Student-$t_3$ shocks reach 17\%. Short, 100-round fitting prefixes under-cover at high persistence; increasing fitted persistence by two estimated standard errors helps only partially. These stress tests do not refute a theorem assuming correct Gaussian dynamics, and they do not validate learned-model confidence merely because some fitted configurations cover.

\paragraph{Wikipedia-calibrated worlds.} The real-dynamics certificate study contains 4,800 runs: 30 articles, six communities, fitted AR(7), horizon 730 days, slates of five or ten, known or fitted dynamics, SP-UCB or successive elimination, and 100 worlds per cell. Gaussian innovations provide the centered reference; independent empirical-residual resampling and weekly block resampling are additional diagnostics. The block stress test violates temporal innovation independence. All pooling widths are oracle widths of the declared mean vector, not learned practical certificates.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/realcert.tex}

Gaussian innovation bounds record zero upper-bound violations in each displayed 100-world cell, but zero of 100 has a Wilson upper failure bound of 3.7\%, not zero population risk. The iid rule records 56/100 violations for five-slot SP-UCB and 94/100 for ten slots, while its mean regret is much lower. Innovation-based elimination retains all 30 arms; iid elimination has 78--99\% upper-bound violations but does not remove the best article in these large-gap Gaussian worlds. The diagnostic does not audit lower-bound coverage or preservation of every top-$k$ arm. Thus invalid coverage need not imply a wrong decision in every environment, and empirical validity does not imply useful rejection or lower finite-horizon regret.

At day 730, a separate five-world random-slate diagnostic reports median arm-interval widths 1.739 for the nuisance plug-in construction, 0.703 for the joint ellipsoid and 0.070 for uncertified pointwise $\pm2$ posterior standard deviations. The best--second gap is 0.135 and the ellipsoid coefficient is about 20. This identifies confidence construction as a practical bottleneck; pointwise posterior precision is not itself an anytime guarantee.

\paragraph{Residual-centering audit.} Empirical residual vectors were not recentered before resampling. Their largest mean coordinate is 0.001534; under the fitted AR polynomial this shifts long-run levels by up to 0.01936 on the rescaled axis. The best article and top-five/top-ten sets remain unchanged, but one of six supplied oracle component widths is too small for the shifted levels. Consequently those resampled cells are implementation diagnostics, not clean nominal-coverage confirmation for the stated fixed means. The Gaussian cells do not have this mean shift. Future resampled replications should center residuals and regenerate certificates and mean-regret targets consistently.

\paragraph{Irreversible failure and finite-horizon regret.} In the controlled bursty-elimination follow-up, iid confidence removes the best arm in 28--89\% of selected high-persistence configurations, leaving 0.14--0.26 loss per subsequent round; innovation confidence records no such removals but often eliminates nothing through 20,000 rounds. A separate two-arm confirmation finds iid failure 30/100 and 47/100 at persistence 0.97 and 0.995 versus zero innovation failures. At the measured horizons the invalid rule can still have lower average regret. These are finite-horizon safety/usefulness tradeoffs; the observed curves alone are not an asymptotic linear-regret proof or evidence that the valid policy eventually wins.

\subsection{Long-run information and spatial contrast checks}

Full autocovariances matter. On the existing 150-article Wikipedia panels, seven-day block-mean variance is 3.6--5.3 times an iid calculation. AR(7)/AR(20) prefix fits predict substantially more of this inflation than a lag-one AR(1) shortcut, which underpredicts long-block inflation by factors 1.2--3.3 in the reported diagnostics. Those cohorts are retrospective; the fits underpredict for roughly two-thirds of articles, and this diagnostic does not calibrate a real-data confidence theorem.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/slates.tex}

The equal-arm $(1-\rho)$ contrast factor is a restricted calculation. With heterogeneous persistence or marginal variance, use the long-run covariance $\Omega$ and contrast variance $\Omega_{ii}+\Omega_{jj}-2\Omega_{ij}$, with finite-calendar corrections when needed. At correlation 0.9, unequal pairs retain factors around 0.28 and 0.38 rather than 0.10. Staggered persistent observations retain substantial covariance benefit in the tested schedules; staggered iid observations retain none. This is schedule-specific evidence, not a theorem that simultaneity is unnecessary in every partial-observation design. Independent Monte Carlo checks over eight equal-pair cells and 20,000 worlds per cell agree with the exact finite-sample variances within 1.8\%.

An exploratory 16-arm factorial holds stationary marginal variance fixed and changes only the learner's graph in corruption controls. At persistence 0.9, correlation strength 0.8 and small mean spread, joint UCB minus matched temporal UCB is $-0.039$ latent-oracle loss per round, paired interval $[-0.070,-0.008]$ over 20 worlds. The larger-spread contrast is inconclusive. Corrupting the learner graph worsens the selected joint-UCB contrasts while holding the true world fixed. This supports a spatial mechanism in a controlled regime; the planned 32--64-arm general-AR, 100-world confirmation remains unfinished.

\subsection{Open Bandit Dataset: a mean-channel application with sparse clicks}

\paragraph{Feasibility and protocol.} The three full uniform-random logs contain 2,691,861 impressions, with 80, 34 and 46 items in the All, Men and Women campaigns. Click rates vary across items; daily dispersion is about twice a binomial working-model expectation, but residual lag-one correlations are $-0.14$ to $-0.22$ by day and approximately zero in six-hour bins. These measurements do not establish a persistent fluctuation channel at the available resolution. Attribute groups come from released retrospective metadata; audience-response groups use days 1--3 only.

\input{\latestExperimentRoot/paper/obd.tex}

The primary application is explicitly semi-synthetic: day 4--7 point estimates define fixed click means and the learner receives simulated binomial feedback. Graph-free empirical-Bayes TS has the lowest observed mean regret among the tested rules, about 0.206 and 0.290 of conservative fixed-level KL-UCB. Oracle pooling yields smaller, graph-dependent gains; standard log-$t$ KL-UCB and ordinary Beta TS are necessary strong controls. Uncertified audience pooling in All never selects the true-best item in the final 10\% of these simulations. This illustrates dilution risk, but is not a real deployment outcome. Prefix ranges cover held-out component ranges in only 25--70\% of the original component instances and cannot be called valid certificates.

\paragraph{Truth uncertainty and drift sensitivity.} A separate audit crosses two campaigns, pooled positions or position 1, and prefixes of two, three or four days, producing 12 splits and 72 graph/width comparisons. With a three-day prefix and pooled positions, the empirically best item's posterior probability of being truly best is 0.627 in All and 0.553 in Women under independent Jeffreys rate posteriors. Thus two million simulated impressions do not make the estimated real-data truth precise. Simultaneous binomial prefix intervals enlarge component widths; for the audience graph, posterior probability that all held-out ranges fit rises from zero to 0.915 in All and 0.686 in Women. These are conditional rate-posterior sensitivities under a binomial working model, not frequentist guarantees against future drift. The audit runs no additional bandit trajectories.

\paragraph{Actual censored-log pilot.} A separate executed pilot contains 142 policy trajectories over the final 72 hours of the Men's position-1 logs. Each learner chooses before the hour, sees only matching randomized clicks/impressions and advances calendar time through empty bins. The first three dates fit the learner, the fourth is development and the last three are test. About 91.7\% of training hourly item bins contain zero clicks, and test trajectories obtain roughly 10--16 clicks. Inverse-propensity summaries are therefore underpowered for a convincing ranking. This pilot is actual logged-feedback evidence for a sparse censored learner; it neither reproduces a deployed full-feedback learner nor validates a Gaussian click filter, permanent-mean PCS or a true latent-state oracle regret.

\subsection{What the updated evidence supports}

The strongest agreement is with conditional mechanisms: useful pooling depends on supplied widths relative to gaps; persistence changes mean information and can invalidate iid confidence under bursty exposure; correlated shocks can reduce particular contrast variances; and prediction, mean learning and final identification require different allocation objectives. No experiment converts a known-model theorem into a fitted-model guarantee. The tested practical certificates are either wide, empirically incomplete, or both. Persistence-aware decisions improve some real-panel comparisons against iid rules, while last-value, EWMA, static ranking and graph-free shrinkage remain essential comparators. We do not claim universal superiority of the proposed algorithms, an established topology advantage on the dynamic panels, or an online platform deployment.

The next method priority is confidence that remains useful after dynamics estimation and simultaneous coverage are charged. The next empirical priority is a broader, predefined chronological Wikipedia panel set and the focused general-AR factorial confirmation. These gaps should govern claims in the condensed paper rather than adding more seeds to already well-covered narrow settings.
